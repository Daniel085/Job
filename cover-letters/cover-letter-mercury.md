Dear Mercury Product Team,

The Interfaces posting describes one surface with many doors: Dashboard, Mobile, Command, the API, and MCP, with shared foundations underneath so a request takes the best path to the work. I have spent the last year building that shape for a harder actor than a customer: an AI agent joining a live phone call.

At Alianza I own the developer-facing side of Crux, the control plane through which AI experiences get access to phone conversations on more than a thousand operators' networks. Before any AI is invoked, Crux applies identity, entitlements, and policy in the network, plays and logs consent, and only then hands the call to an agent or streams media to a post-call partner. The customer-control questions in your posting, consequential actions, permissions, and visibility into what an agent did, are the questions I ship against every week. I designed the access model and API standards every Alianza engineering team builds against, shipped the Crux API 1.0 in September, and built the MCP server for it.

On AI quality, the posting asks how it was evaluated, where it failed, and what changed. The documentation behind developer.alianza.com is generated by AI agents from the API definition and reviewed by humans. The design rule that made it trustworthy was simple: where the definition leaves something unexplained, the system says so instead of guessing. That rule came from failures. I have written publicly about the hazards of trusting systems designed to make things up, and about putting agents on the two ends of product discovery that people do badly, prep and synthesis, while keeping humans in the conversation.

On breadth of surface: at Vonage I owned client SDKs across eight platforms and shipped Video Express, a simplified video product that went from prototype to GA in under nine months during 10x platform growth, built by watching what customers were actually trying to do with the SDK. At Yahoo I owned Mail Monetization for a consumer audience. At Twilio I directed SendGrid's move from a single email API to a multi-channel platform.

One gap I want to name rather than paper over. The posting asks for five years leading PMs. I have not held a line-management title. At Vonage I mentored and onboarded a team of five PMs, built the team's frameworks and ways of working, and was the lead PM the others organized around. At Alianza I set the standards other teams build to. If Mercury needs a seasoned people manager on day one, that is a real difference. If it needs someone who can set direction across five surfaces, raise the bar on AI quality, and grow a team from that base, I am confident in the record.

I would welcome a conversation about what Interfaces needs in its first two quarters.

Daniel O'Rorke
